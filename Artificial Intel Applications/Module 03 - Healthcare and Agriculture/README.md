# Module 3 – AI in Healthcare & AI in Agriculture

## Key Concepts
**Healthcare:**
- AI-driven diagnostics: medical image analysis, NLP for clinical notes, wearables/IoT for continuous monitoring
- Personalized medicine: genomic analysis, drug response prediction, clinical decision support
- Drug discovery: accelerating pipelines, predicting drug-target interactions, designing/repurposing molecules
- Healthcare operations: predictive analytics for resource management, AI scheduling, insurance fraud/claims processing
- Mental health AI: therapy chatbots, risk assessment models, speech/facial-expression analysis for early detection

**Agriculture:**
- Precision agriculture: data-driven, site-specific farming using Variable Rate Technology (VRT)
- Crop and soil monitoring via remote sensing and hyperspectral imaging
- Livestock management and aquaculture using computer vision and predictive analytics
- Agricultural robotics: autonomous tractors, harvesting, weeding/pruning robots
- Vertical farming and controlled-environment agriculture
- Sustainability: carbon sequestration, biodiversity monitoring, food waste reduction

## Tools & Technologies
- Medical imaging ML models, NLP for clinical documentation, wearable/IoT health devices
- Blue River's "See & Spray" (90% less herbicide use), Prospera (AI crop health monitoring)
- Drones and hyperspectral sensors for crop/soil analysis

## Real-World Examples
- Moderna used AI to accelerate mRNA COVID-19 vaccine design and production
- Blue River and Prospera as precision-agriculture case studies

## Ethical Considerations
**Healthcare:** data privacy/security, bias and equitable access to care, maintaining human oversight of the doctor-patient relationship, need for AI explainability under regulation

**Agriculture:** data ownership and privacy, the digital divide in access to AI farming technology, environmental impact of AI-powered agriculture, ensuring benefits reach smallholder farmers fairly

## Key Takeaway
Both healthcare and agriculture show the same pattern: AI delivers real efficiency and precision gains, but only when paired with deliberate attention to who has access to the technology and who bears the risk when it fails.
