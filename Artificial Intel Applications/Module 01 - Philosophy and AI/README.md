# Module 1 – Philosophy and AI & Ethical and Societal Implications of AI

## Key Concepts
- Philosophy as a lens for AI: ontology (what is AI's nature?), epistemology (how does AI "know" things?), and ethics (what should AI do?)
- Consciousness and intelligence debates: the Turing Test, Searle's Chinese Room argument, and the "hard problem" of consciousness
- Theories of mind applied to AI: dualism, materialism, and functionalism
- Philosophical movements underpinning AI development: rationalism, empiricism, and existentialism
- AI's impact on human identity, privacy, and social norms
- The future of work: automation, job displacement, and policy responses (UBI, retraining)

## Tools & Technologies
- No specific AI tools/platforms covered — this module is conceptual and philosophical rather than hands-on

## Real-World Examples
- Autonomous vehicle ethical dilemmas (trolley-problem-style decision-making)
- GDPR as a case study in AI governance and policy

## Ethical Considerations
- Sources of bias in AI (historical data, developer assumptions) and fairness measures (demographic parity, equal opportunity, calibration)
- Balancing innovation with regulation; the role of public policy in ethical AI deployment

## Key Takeaway
Philosophy provides the vocabulary and frameworks (ethics, consciousness, epistemology) needed to ask the right questions about AI — before getting into the technical "how," this module establishes the "should we, and how do we know."
